# Tabletop playtest 2026-11-16: printed cards, two humans

Played with the physical board and printed *New Notions* cards (v0.1). Two players
taking turns as clue-giver and guesser: B gave the clues for card 5 and Forrest
guessed; Forrest gave the clues for card 6 and B guessed. Per-prompt results are still to come
(see below).

Session notes:
- **Overall a success.** The cards worked and we enjoyed playing them.
- **Two players is not typical.** With only one guesser there's one person to spot
  associations, so turns stall more easily. Three or more players is more fun and
  less likely to get stuck. Read the difficulty results with that in mind.
- **Many prompts were harder than expected.** We skipped some prompts and failed
  others. A prompt can look easy at a glance ("how hard can that be?"), but in play
  it's hard to choose the icons that build it, or the guesser doesn't make the
  association the clue-giver needs.
- **Clueability and a working route matter most for difficulty, and you can't
  judge them by reading the prompt.** You have to work through choosing clues and
  guessing them. One idea: simulate play with LLMs and calibrate the results against
  human playtests.
- **House rule: cubes on picture details.** Our group sometimes places a cube on a
  specific spot inside an icon's picture rather than in the box beside it, to point at
  one element: the octopus in A4 or the lightbulb in A8. This may not be in the
  official rules. Prompts shouldn't depend on it to be solvable. But it makes prompts
  that are drawn inside an icon trivial, so those are now ruled out (an *Octopus* or
  *Lightbulb* prompt). Composite prompts built from a pictured element are fine:
  *Lighthouse* = the lightbulb in A8 + a cube on B14.

### Card 5 observations

- **Two skips, one in Blue.** Taco shows that a Blue prompt can still be one the
  clue-giver can't see how to start.
- **Picture details were used on 5 of 9 prompts.** For this group they're a normal
  part of play, not an edge case.
- **Clue-givers didn't follow our recorded routes.** Drone and Couch potato
  succeeded by other routes. Statue of Liberty and Elvis Presley failed without
  trying the recorded ones. A recorded route shows that a prompt can be solved, not
  that a clue-giver will find that route.
- **Idioms can be clued by meaning.** Couch potato was solved from "lazy person at
  home watching TV" without building either word.

### Card 6 observations

- **Both Blue failures left out a distinctive link.** Popcorn was described by how
  it's made, and Cinema went unused. Snail used Slow, but not the shell as a house.
  C3's turtle picture may also have pointed to a neighbor. For Blue, the strongest
  association matters more than an accurate description.
- **Idioms split by how directly their words map to icons.** *Once in a blue moon*
  (every word is an icon) went quickly. *Rain check* failed even though it was
  framed as an expression: the written check didn't come through, and the meaning
  was never clued. This fits the band standards: *Once in a
  blue moon* belongs in Red, and *Rain check* in Black or harder.
- **Spatial placement is part of how this group plays.** Lighthouse used picture
  details, and Lego used an arrangement of cubes. Both are fine in play, but a
  prompt shouldn't need them.
