# Prompt guidelines

How to choose and grade prompts for the expansion. Revise this file as playtesting
shows what works. Back to [README](../README.md).

## Every prompt must pass these tests

1. **Recognition.** Most American adults today would know right away what it is
   without needing it explained. Roughly: at least 4 of 5 typical players for
   Blue and Red, at least 3 of 5 for Black.
2. **Clueability.** It fits under an obvious Main Concept icon (Object, Fauna,
   Food, Character, Title, Expression, and so on) **and** has at least two other
   features you can show with separate icons. Check against
   [board-icons.md](board-icons.md).
3. **Distinctness.** A clue-giver can tell it apart from nearby answers. Example:
   "glasses" vs. "monocle" works because the number of circles is clueable.
4. **Durability.** It should still be familiar in 5 to 10 years. Recent pop culture
   is welcome if it has already lasted or crossed into the mainstream (e.g. a
   franchise, an app everyone uses, a widely known star). Avoid memes, single-season
   hits, and short-lived celebrities.
5. **Family-friendly.** Nothing crude, sexual, hateful, or likely to upset people at
   a mixed table.
6. **Fits on the card.** Keep it short. The limit is about 24 characters for now;
   adjust once the card layout exists.
7. **Not a base-game duplicate**, including obvious variants (singular/plural, small
   wording changes).

## Difficulty bands

All three bands use the same board. Difficulty comes from how much decomposition the
clue needs, not from how obscure the answer is.

| Band | What fits | Examples (from the rules) |
|---|---|---|
| **Blue: easy** | Common concrete things. A Main Concept plus a few clues gets it. | Bee, Glasses |
| **Red: difficult** | Compound ideas, specific people/characters/places/titles, or things that need a Sub-Concept or careful icon order. | Fire station, Zombie, Stethoscope |
| **Black: most difficult** | Familiar phrases, idioms, titles, and abstract ideas that must be built from several parts. | (Style:) Break the ice, Back to square one |

Lessons from playtesting:

- **Direct compounds are Blue.** If each part of a prompt has its own obvious icon,
  it's easy, whatever its form. *Food truck* (vehicle + food) went in one step. A
  prompt can be Red when an icon only hints at a part (Spider-Man: the grid looks a bit
  like a web, but it takes a moment to see it).
- **Hard to show is fine for Red and Black.** Ideas the board has no direct icon for
  (the "self" in *Selfie*, the "ice" in *Break the ice*) make good challenges, as long
  as players know the prompt immediately once it's guessed. Figuring out how to reach
  the idea is part of the fun.

## Good prompt types

- Everyday objects, animals, foods, places, jobs, activities.
- Modern compounds people actually say: *video call*, *delivery driver*, *escape room*.
- Enduring characters, people, films, books, and brands.
- Common idioms whose words suggest concrete icons: *hit the road*,
  *under the weather*, *break the ice*.

## Avoid

- Proverbs, literary quotations, and phrases where the exact wording is the whole
  challenge.
- Long wordings of a simple idea. Use a multi-word phrase only when it's a set
  phrase people always say exactly that way (*Break the ice*). Otherwise trim it to
  its core: *Snooze button*, not *Hit the snooze button*. Guessers find the idea
  quickly and then get stuck on the exact words.
- Archaic, regional, or specialist vocabulary.
- Things with only one clueable feature (purely abstract single words with no
  concrete angle).
- Near-synonyms of a prompt already on the same card.

## Balancing a card

- The nine prompts on a card should come from different categories (not three
  animals in the Blue band).
- Each band should be about as hard as the same band on other cards.
