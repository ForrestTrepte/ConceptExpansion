# New English-Language Cards for *Concept*: Expansion and Prompt-Source Research

## Bottom line

There is **still no verified official English-language card-only expansion for the original 2013 *Concept***. Repos Production did publish a genuine 110-card expansion containing 990 new prompts, *Concept: Neue Begriffe*, but the verified commercial edition is German; a corresponding French edition, *Concept: La Recharge*, also exists. The current English Repos page does not list either as an English product, and Asmodee UK's current Repos catalog lists the base game, *Concept Kids: Animals*, and the forthcoming *Concept Stories*, but not an English prompt-card expansion. citeturn427904search3turn276352search1turn427904search2turn702963search6

For your stated goal—**modern, commonly recognized, readily guessable words and phrases**—the best sources I found are not obscure *Concept* fan decks. The strongest combination is:

| Priority | Source | Why it is useful for *Concept* | Main drawback |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | [Open Party Game Word Lists](https://github.com/ylwl1997/party-game-word-lists) | Clean CC BY 4.0 JSON/TXT; deliberately selected for guessability; celebrities, movies, TV, places, animals, food, funny concepts, sports/actions | Only 172+ prompts at present |
| **2** | [Monikers Print & Play](https://boardgamegeek.com/filepage/109438/monikers-print-and-play-pdf) | 485 names/prompts designed for guessing; excellent people, pop culture, characters, concepts; legally reusable noncommercially | Some adult humor, dated references, and culturally niche cards |
| **3** | [OpenWord Lexicon](https://github.com/macshonle/openword-lexicon) | Huge pool with frequency, concreteness, part-of-speech and source/license metadata—ideal for automatically generating easy/medium word lists | Mostly single words, not curated phrases |
| **4** | [English idiom datasets](https://github.com/tema4p/english-vocabulary) plus [MAGPIE](https://github.com/hslh/magpie-corpus) | Good raw material for replacing the game's archaic expressions with familiar idioms | Must filter aggressively for familiarity and literal clueability |
| **5** | [Official Concept Print & Play](https://print-and-play.asmodee.fun/en/game/concept) | Official, English, immediately compatible | Demo rather than a real expansion; only 30 preselected challenges |
| **6** | *Concept Kids: Animals* | 110 additional official animal targets | Narrow category; standalone children's edition, not an expansion |
| **7** | Other commercial guessing games such as Codenames/Pictionary/Telestrations/Time's Up | Their cards are mechanically compatible sources of targets | Usually copyrighted; better used by physically drawing their cards than copied into a homemade deck |

The first four sources can support a genuinely better replacement deck than the original if you apply frequency, familiarity, concreteness, age, and "iconability" filters rather than simply dumping thousands of English words onto cards. The open party-game dataset explicitly asks contributors to keep entries recognizable, family-friendly, short, and nonduplicative, while OpenWord exposes exactly the metadata needed for a more systematic filter. citeturn251355search5turn251355search9

## Official and commercial *Concept* material

The original English *Concept* contains **110 cards and 990 concepts**, arranged across three difficulty levels. Repos describes the targets broadly as objects, characters, titles, words, and expressions; the modern rules likewise say players choose a "word, title or expression." citeturn427904search8turn719351search9

### The genuine expansion exists, but not in verified English

[*Concept: Neue Begriffe*](https://www.asmodee.de/produkte/concept-neue-begriffe) is a true expansion, not a standalone edition. It contains **110 new cards with 990 previously unpublished prompts**, again divided into easy, difficult, and challenge categories, and explicitly requires the base game. Asmodee Germany still lists it as an expansion. citeturn277990search0turn277990search2

A 2020 BGG news item reported both German *Neue Begriffe* and French *Concept: La Recharge*, while commenters were explicitly hoping for an English version. I found no later official evidence that such an English release happened. The present English Repos page shows only the base game, *Concept Kids: Animals*, and Concept XL under "versions," whereas the German page specifically includes *Neue Begriffe*. That makes "no official English card expansion located" a strong conclusion rather than proof of nonexistence in every market. citeturn276352search1turn427904search0turn427904search2

Interestingly, the German expansion appears to contain some noticeably more contemporary material than the 2013 deck: its published rules/examples include targets such as **"Escape Room"** alongside ordinary concrete nouns. That suggests Repos itself recognized the need for fresher vocabulary, but buying the German deck would require translating every card. citeturn277990search8

### *Concept Kids: Animals* is compatible in spirit, but not an expansion

[*Concept Kids: Animals*](https://www.rprod.com/en/games/concept-kids) is a **standalone cooperative adaptation for ages four and up** with 110 animal cards, its own board, rings, card holder, rules, and bestiary. It is therefore not an expansion to the adult game. citeturn582818search1

You could nevertheless use its animal targets with the adult board. The disadvantages are obvious: every target is an animal, many would fall into the original game's easy range, and you would probably encounter duplicates or near-duplicates with the existing 990 prompts. It is useful as a themed mini-deck, not as your main solution. citeturn582818search1turn427904search8

### *Concept Stories* is new but serves a different purpose

A genuinely new official product, [*Concept Stories*](https://www.asmodee.co.uk/collections/repos), is now appearing in English-language catalogs. It is a **standalone cooperative game** built around 110 unusual true stories that players reconstruct in three stages using Concept-style icons. Canadian and retail listings currently put the English release around **Q1 2027**, so as of November 16, 2026 it should be treated as forthcoming rather than generally available. citeturn702963search0turn702963search3

It is not a substitute for ordinary word cards: the targets are multi-part anecdotes rather than single concepts. It may, however, become an interesting source of harder "event" challenges once released. citeturn702963search2turn702963search5

### The official Print & Play is useful, but small

Asmodee still hosts an English [official *Concept* Print & Play](https://print-and-play.asmodee.fun/en/game/concept). BGG describes this pandemic-era version as a **30-card demo with preselected clues**, where players reconstruct answers from icon arrangements. It is official and free, but it does not provide hundreds of new blank prompt cards. citeturn817885search0turn241858view0

I would download it chiefly as a reference for card dimensions/layout or for 30 extra challenge situations, not as the expansion you are looking for.

## Fan-created and community material

The *Concept* community has repeatedly run into exactly your problem. BGG's current Variants forum includes threads titled **"More words," "Concept companion web app," "When you know (almost) all the cards," "Our super fun variant – Simpsons Concept,"** and, more recently, **"PSA wikicept."** The forum index confirms that these resources exist, but BGG's indexing did not expose enough of the underlying thread contents to verify their complete word lists or licenses, so I would treat them as **leads, not vetted downloadable decks**. [BGG Concept Variants forum](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/147151/concept/forums/69). citeturn608120search0turn276352search0

A particularly relevant [2019 Reddit discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/boardgames/comments/cif5ca) asks what other games can function as *Concept* expansions after players memorize the cards. Respondents specifically suggest **Codenames, Monikers, Time's Up: Title Recall, Timeline, Pictionary, and Telestrations**; one commenter makes the important distinction that ordinary random-word generators miss the celebrities, expressions, movies, and named entities that give *Concept* variety. citeturn811315reddit8

That Reddit thread aligns well with how the game itself is designed: official *Concept* material spans ordinary nouns, people, titles, and expressions, not just dictionary entries. citeturn427904search8

I also found a 2026 Etsy [Concept card-template product](https://www.etsy.com/listing/1806712992/concept-board-game-template-canva) supplying editable PDFs/Canva-style customization rather than new prompt content. It could simplify home printing, but I would not rely on it for expansion vocabulary, and its use of *Concept* branding/art should not be assumed to grant any Repos IP rights. citeturn811315search2

One useful warning: a Hugging Face dataset called [Concept](https://huggingface.co/datasets/IneG/concept) contains actual target names and game logs harvested from Board Game Arena, including original difficulty labels and card identifiers. It is **gated and subject to noncommercial conditions**. Because it appears capable of reconstructing much of the publisher's target set, I would **not** use it as a convenient source for republishing the official deck. It is more appropriate for research, and perhaps—subject to its terms—for checking whether a candidate duplicates a base-game item. citeturn719351search4

## Open and reusable prompt sources

### Open Party Game Word Lists is the cleanest starting point

The strongest ready-made open source I found is [ylwl1997/party-game-word-lists](https://github.com/ylwl1997/party-game-word-lists). It currently contains **172+ prompts** divided into celebrities/famous names, movies, TV shows, places/landmarks, animals, food/drink, funny/absurd concepts, and sports/actions. Downloads are supplied both as category-level `.txt`/`.json` files and a combined JSON/flat text list. citeturn251355search5

More importantly, its contribution guidelines explicitly call for prompts that are **guessable, well known, family-friendly by default, one to three words long, and nonduplicative**. That is almost exactly the editorial brief you gave for your replacement cards. It is licensed **CC BY 4.0**, so you can legally modify, remix, and print a derived deck with attribution. citeturn251355search5

Because it is small, I would use nearly all of the clean entries that your group recognizes, then supplement them from other sources.

### Monikers is excellent for names, characters, phenomena, and pop culture

The official-author-uploaded [Monikers Print & Play on BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/filepage/109438/monikers-print-and-play-pdf) contains **485 English cards**. The game's entire premise is recognizing named people, characters, memes, objects, and cultural references under increasingly restrictive clue rules, so its targets tend to be unusually compatible with *Concept*. citeturn258062search0turn258062search2

The older Monikers rules state that the game is available under **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**, permitting sharing and adaptation with attribution, noncommercial use, and ShareAlike; the *Monikers* name itself remains trademarked. citeturn564032search5turn564032search6

There are two reasons to filter it heavily. First, BGG classifies the game as mature/adult, and the present commercial edition is explicitly marketed for ages 17+. Second, many cards depend on specific internet/pop-cultural knowledge, which makes them fun for the right group but risks recreating the "obscure reference" problem in a newer form. citeturn258062search2turn564032search1

For your use, I would keep only entries that at least, say, **four of five people in your group recognize instantly when shown the answer**. That one test matters more than whether a reference is objectively "famous."

### Cards Against Humanity is legally reusable but editorially weak for this purpose

Cards Against Humanity officially provides its base game as a free download and licenses it under **CC BY-NC-SA 2.0**. The publisher explicitly permits copying and modification for noncommercial purposes with attribution and ShareAlike. [Official download page](https://www.cardsagainsthumanity.com/). citeturn251355search0turn251355search10

Mechanically, however, it is a poor primary source. Many white cards are sentence fragments, jokes, sexual material, or deliberately absurd constructions rather than visually decomposable concepts. A small hand-curated subset can work, but Monikers provides much higher yield.

### OpenWord is the best generator for common single words

[OpenWord Lexicon](https://github.com/macshonle/openword-lexicon) is particularly promising if you want to generate hundreds of fresh cards algorithmically. Its current build describes roughly **1.35 million lexical entries**, with metadata including frequency tiers, concreteness, parts of speech, register/profanity-style labels, and per-word source/licensing information. It can be filtered specifically to common, concrete, game-appropriate vocabulary. citeturn251355search9

Licensing is tracked per source rather than applying one blanket license to every datum: the repository identifies sources including Wiktionary under CC BY-SA, EOWL under a permissive dictionary license, WordNet under CC BY, and research datasets with more restrictive conditions. If you plan only a private home deck, that complexity is modest; if you intend to publicly redistribute the generated word list, use OpenWord's source filters rather than treating every entry as equally permissive. citeturn251355search9

This source is especially useful for filling categories *Concept* handles brilliantly: occupations, household objects, foods, animals, activities, transportation, technology, places, emotions, weather, and common abstract concepts.

### Wordfreq is better as a scoring tool than a deck source

[wordfreq](https://github.com/rspeer/wordfreq) provides language-frequency estimates and is freely redistributable under Apache licensing for the code, with data components operating under CC BY-SA-style terms and source-specific attribution requirements. citeturn251355search6turn251355search7

Its greatest value here is **screening**: given candidate words from other datasets, reject anything with unusually low modern English frequency. I would not export its database wholesale; the project itself cautions that simply converting its data to CSV can violate its attribution model. citeturn251355search7

### Idiom datasets can fix the weakest part of the original deck

For phrases, [tema4p/english-vocabulary](https://github.com/tema4p/english-vocabulary) includes English idioms and phrasal verbs in plain UTF-8 files, with content under **CC BY-NC 4.0**. citeturn309316search0

[MAGPIE](https://github.com/hslh/magpie-corpus) is much larger, containing **1,756 potentially idiomatic expression types** and more than 56,000 annotated usage instances derived from the British National Corpus. It is research-oriented rather than party-game-curated, but it provides a rich phrase inventory from which familiar expressions can be selected. citeturn309316search5

A newer English-idiom dataset, [baiango/english_idioms](https://github.com/baiango/english_idioms), has 500+ entries in CSV form, but its author says they were generated using ChatGPT. That makes it useful as a brainstorming source, not my first choice for authoritative commonness judgments. citeturn309316search1

For this category I would explicitly **exclude proverbs, literary quotations, maxim-style sayings, and anything whose recognition depends on knowing the exact wording**. Those are precisely the prompts that age badly and produce the failure mode you described.

## What I would actually put on new cards

A good expansion should not merely be "new." It should preserve *Concept*'s key pleasure: the answer feels obvious **after** someone sees the right icon combination.

I would build roughly **60% concrete/common concepts, 20% people/titles/pop culture, and 20% phrases/compound concepts**, rather than reproducing the original deck's tendency to escalate difficulty by reaching for less familiar material. This mix is my recommendation rather than an official ratio.

Here are examples I would consider strong modern prompts. They are examples of the kind of material I would select or write, not a transcription of any commercial deck:

| Easier | Medium | Hard but fair |
|---|---|---|
| wireless earbuds | group chat | read the room |
| QR code | video call | spill the tea |
| food delivery | escape room | on the same page |
| electric scooter | streaming service | elephant in the room |
| podcast | smart speaker | go down a rabbit hole |
| selfie | password | break the ice |
| drone | remote work | under the weather |
| emoji | cloud storage | better late than never |
| food truck | ride share | go with the flow |
| smartwatch | viral video | hit the road |
| recycling bin | online shopping | mixed signals |
| charging cable | air fryer | a win-win |
| traffic jam | subscription | out of the loop |
| board game | screen time | call it a day |
| delivery driver | voice message | back to square one |

These work better than obscure proverbs because most can be decomposed into **multiple independent Concept-board dimensions**—object/person/action, technology, communication, location, size, color, time, emotion, movement, relationship, etc.—rather than requiring the guesser to recall an exact quotation.

For proper nouns and titles, I would avoid building a static "2026 celebrities" deck, which will date quickly. Instead make a **rotating pop-culture module** of perhaps 60–100 prompts that you are willing to replace every few years. Monikers is deliberately built around recognizable names and is therefore a much better seed source for that module than a general dictionary. citeturn258062search0turn564032search1

The same principle applies to difficulty. Hard prompts should be difficult because they require **clever decomposition**, not because nobody knows what the answer means. The base game's own mechanics support that distinction: all three levels use the same icon vocabulary, and difficult answers are supposed to demand more elaborate combinations and sub-concepts. citeturn719351search9

## A reproducible filtering and printing workflow

I would build the deck as a spreadsheet with one row per candidate prompt and columns for `prompt`, `source`, `source_license`, `category`, `recognition`, `frequency`, `iconability`, `datedness`, `adult_content`, `existing_deck_duplicate`, and `difficulty`.

Start by importing the CC BY party-game list in full, then add filtered Monikers targets, 300–500 common concrete nouns/actions from OpenWord, and 100–200 idioms from an open phrase source. Keep provenance attached to every row so attribution remains simple. The first two sources already provide highly usable machine-readable or printable formats, while OpenWord provides source-aware metadata for filtering. citeturn251355search5turn258062search0turn251355search9

Then apply the following scoring rule:

**Recognition score, 0–5.** Ask five representative players "Do you know exactly what this is?" without asking them to explain it. Require at least **4/5 for easy**, **4/5 for medium**, and **3/5 for hard**. This single test eliminates most generational and subculture traps.

**Iconability score, 0–3.** Give one point if the prompt has an obvious main category on the Concept board, one if it has at least two secondary attributes that can be represented independently, and one if a clue-giver can plausibly distinguish it from nearby concepts. Reject zeroes and usually ones.

**Modernity penalty.** Reject phrases that sound proverbial, literary, regional, occupationally specialized, or whose natural use is declining. For ordinary words, use word-frequency metadata from OpenWord/wordfreq as a warning signal rather than as an absolute cutoff. citeturn251355search9turn251355search7

**Pop-culture half-life.** Keep enduring characters, works, and people in the main deck; put memes, influencers, current shows and fleeting phenomena in a separate replaceable deck.

**Phrase rule.** Prefer phrases whose individual words evoke concrete icons—*"break the ice," "hit the road," "under the weather"*—over aphorisms whose exact wording is the whole challenge. That preserves visual wordplay without turning Concept into a quotation quiz.

**Duplicate check.** Enter the prompts from your physical *Concept* deck into a private spreadsheet and normalize them by lowercasing, stripping punctuation, and collapsing singular/plural variants. I would avoid scraping or redistributing the gated BGA-derived dataset solely to reconstruct the copyrighted official prompt list. citeturn719351search4

For card construction, the existing *Concept* cards are approximately **56.5 × 87.5 mm**, according to BGG's component listing. A practical homemade card can therefore hold nine prompts in three difficulty bands exactly like the original. citeturn811315search0

For layout, [Decklab](https://decklab.ai/) can import a spreadsheet, create one card per row, and export print-and-play PDFs with cut marks; it also supports poker/bridge/custom card dimensions and individual PNG export. citeturn811315search4 An open-source alternative is [BoardForge](https://github.com/TabletopFoundry/boardforge), which supports CSV import and print-and-play PDF export under an MIT license. citeturn719351search2

For home printing, I would **not copy the official card artwork**. Use plain text, three colored difficulty headings, and a neutral back—or sleeve homemade fronts in opaque sleeves with spare playing cards. That avoids unnecessary trademark/artwork complications while giving you mechanically identical cards. The underlying words themselves can then carry only the licensing obligations of the datasets you actually used.

If you want a target size, I would aim initially for **100 cards × nine prompts = 900 new targets**, then playtest only 20–25 cards before printing the full set. A good first pool might contain roughly 450 common words/actions, 180 proper names/titles, 150 contemporary compounds, and 120 genuinely common phrases. After several sessions, delete anything that repeatedly produces "I've never heard of that" rather than "I can't believe we didn't get that."

The most defensible licensing combination for a publicly shared version would be to make your own prompt selections from **CC BY 4.0 party-game data plus appropriately filtered open lexical sources**, and either omit Monikers or release the resulting deck under terms compatible with its **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** license. For a deck used only by your own group, the practical copyright risk is far simpler, but commercial redistribution is a different question. citeturn251355search5turn564032search5turn251355search9

## Recommended shortlist

If the goal is to improve *Concept* immediately rather than collect every possible resource, I would proceed in this order.

**Best ready-made source: Open Party Game Word Lists.** Download the flat list and category JSON, remove anything your group dislikes, and use essentially everything that passes a recognition check. Its license, format, categories, and editorial standard are unusually well aligned with this project. citeturn251355search5

**Best large guessing-game source: Monikers PnP.** Extract the card names, ignore the explanatory clue text, discard adult/obscure/dated entries, and expect perhaps a minority rather than all 485 to survive. It is particularly valuable because it fills the "people, characters, titles, internet culture" side that dictionary datasets cannot. citeturn258062search0turn564032search5

**Best way to generate hundreds of ordinary words: OpenWord.** Filter for high-frequency concrete nouns, common verbs and familiar adjectives, then manually retain entries that interact well with Concept's icon board. citeturn251355search9

**Best phrase approach: build your own curated subset from open idiom corpora.** Do not copy hundreds indiscriminately. Familiarity matters much more than corpus size. MAGPIE's 1,756 expression types and CC/noncommercial idiom repositories are useful candidate generators, not finished party-game decks. citeturn309316search5turn309316search0

**Best commercial workaround: use another game's cards physically.** Monikers, Pictionary/Telestrations-style cards, Time's Up, Codenames and similar word games can simply sit beside *Concept* as a draw deck. Community players already report doing exactly this. It solves the copyright-copying problem because you are using the product you bought rather than reproducing its contents. citeturn811315reddit8

**Official products are secondary.** *Neue Begriffe* is exactly the expansion you want structurally, but not in verified English; *Concept Kids* is too narrow; *Concept Stories* is a different game and is not expected until 2027; and the official PnP is only a 30-challenge demo. citeturn427904search3turn582818search1turn702963search3turn817885search0
